The Five Milestones

The breeding journey, done right.

Breeding is biology, not a dice roll. We walk you through every critical phase with diagnostic precision and honest ethical guardrails.

01
Pre-Mating Fitness

Pre-breeding health check & blood clearance

Before introducing a stud, we run a full physical and reproductive exam on both parents. We screen for endemic Kenyan tick fevers (Babesia & Ehrlichia) which trigger sudden early resorption or aborted litters.

✓ Ovarian palpation • Testicular motility • Tick-fever panel
02
Genetics & Radiographs

Genetic screening & KKC hip/elbow scoring

Protect your kennel line for generations. We perform standardized sedated pelvic and elbow X-rays evaluated under Kenya Kennel Club (KKC) and FCI international standards, plus DNA profiling for hereditary diseases.

✓ Digital radiology • KKC score submissions • Degenerative myelopathy checks
03
Ovulation Timing

Heat tracking & quantitative progesterone timing

Counting "day 11 or 13" from bleeding is the #1 reason Kenyan matings miss. We perform serial in-house quantitative progesterone assays to pinpoint the exact 48-hour fertile window for natural mating or AI.

✓ 2-hour blood results • Cytology swabs • Peak LH surge pinpointing
04
Prenatal Ultrasound

Pregnancy scans & whelping theatre standby

Day 28 to 35 high-resolution ultrasound confirms viable gestational sacs and fetal heart rates. At Day 55, radiographic skeletal counts confirm exact puppy numbers so you never leave an unborn pup inside during whelping.

✓ Doppler fetal monitoring • Late-term pelvic sizing • 24/7 C-section team
05
Neonates & Pedigrees

Puppy care: vaccines, microchips & paperwork

From tube feeding weak neonates on Day 1 to the 6th and 8th-week Parvo/DHPP vaccinations. We install ISO microchips, issue official vet health passes, and verify pedigree documentation for your buyers.

✓ ISO 11784 microchips • Export certs • Buyer health guarantee slips

Ethical Vet Checklist

Before you breed:
the honest realities.

We refuse to treat dogs like puppy printing machines. Here are the clinical facts most breeders only learn after an expensive disaster.

📋 Kenyan Breeder's Code of Ethics
Have a whelping emergency?

If your dam has been actively straining for over 45 minutes with no pup produced, call our triage line immediately.

📞 Call +254 712 345 678

Never on her first heat. A bitch's first season occurs between 6 and 10 months, when her own growth plates are still fusing and she is psychologically a pup herself.

For giant and mastiff breeds common in Kenya (Boerboel, Cane Corso, German Shepherd, Ridgeback), we strongly mandate waiting until the 3rd heat, or at least 20 to 24 months of age. This allows her hips and elbows to be definitively X-rayed and scored before pregnancy stress.

Vet Rule: A dam bred under 18 months has a 3.4x higher incidence of uterine inertia requiring emergency caesarean section.

While modern reproductive biology allows back-to-back breeding in young, peak-fitness dams under strict clinical supervision, this requires mandatory uterine ultrasound clearance post-weaning.

In Kenya's climate with prevalent tick challenges, we recommend the skip-a-heat cycle. After nursing a large litter of 8–12 pups, a dam's body condition score, red blood cell count, and calcium reserves need a minimum of 4 to 6 months to replenish fully.

Many novice breeders look only at puppy sale prices without budgeting for clinical overhead:

  • Progesterone & Pre-breeding checks: KES 12,000 – KES 18,000
  • Prenatal scans & skeletal X-rays: KES 14,000
  • Emergency C-Section Emergency Buffer: KES 35,000 – KES 45,000 (must be set aside)
  • Litter puppy food, weaning mush & supplements: KES 25,000+
  • Puppy microchips, 6 & 8-week vaccines (x8 pups): KES 24,000
If you don't have at least KES 60,000 liquid reserves in your M-Pesa before mating, do not breed. Whelping emergencies don't wait for payday.

We hold a zero-tolerance policy on passing on crippling genetic defects. We will advise against breeding if:

  • Dam or sire tests positive for Brucella canis (incurable zoonotic bacterium).
  • X-rays display severe Grade 3 or 4 Hip Dysplasia or ununited anconeal process.
  • The dog has severe idiopathic epilepsy or recurring hereditary demodicosis.
  • Unstable or overly aggressive temperament unsuitable for working/family homes.
